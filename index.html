Letonic Grammar and Basic Vocabulary

Letonic is the historical language of the Leton people. Historically, there are only two forms of the language: Old Letonic and Modern Letonic. Modern Letonic was deliberately reconstructed to reproduce Old Letonic almost completely, preserving its grammar, vocabulary, phonology, and orthography.

2. Phonology

Letonic has five basic vowels:

LetterIPA
a/a/
e/e/
i/i/
o/o/
u/u/

Diphthongs such as ai, au, ei and oi also occur.

Consonants: p b t d k g ʔ m n ŋ s h w j r l c.

ŋ is like the ng in English sing; j is like the y in English yes.

The basic syllable structure is (C)V(C). Complex word-initial consonant clusters are generally absent.

3. Nouns and Number

Singular nouns are unmarked. The plural suffix is -n.

awa — person
awan — people
kuma — dog
kuman — dogs

After numerals, the plural suffix may be omitted:

ni kuma — two dogs

8. Adjectives

Adjectives normally follow nouns and do not agree in number or case.

tuma mar — big house
kuma pi — small dog
wari sai — good/clean water

Comparative: more + adjective
Superlative: max + adjective

11. Negation

The negative particle is ne.

ne ŋa-kal — I do not see
pa ne ŋa-kal — I did not see
ka ne ŋa-kal — I will not see

12. Basic Word Order

Letonic uses SOV word order.

ŋa kuma-m kal. — I see the dog.
i wari-m su. — He/she drinks water.
ma tuma-m kar. — We build a house.

13. Location and Direction

tuma-na — at/in the house
tuma-ta — to the house
tuma-ma — from the house
ŋa tuma-na naŋ. — I sleep at home.
i wama-ta pal. — He/she goes to the sea.
i mara-ma kali. — He/she comes from the land.

14. Copula

The copula is an. It is often omitted in the present tense.

ŋa Leton-man. — I am Leton.
ŋa Leton-man an. — I am Leton.
ŋa Leton-man pa an. — I was Leton.
ŋa Leton-man ka an. — I will be Leton.

15. Questions and Commands

The sentence-final particle ya marks questions.

ti Leton-man ya? — Are you Leton?
ti wari-m su ya? — Do you drink water?

The bare verb root forms an imperative:

pal! — Go!
kal! — Look!
pal-sa. — Please go.
kali-sa. — Please come.

21. Cultural Semantics

wari “water” became a culturally important polysemous word associated with water, rivers, flow, movement, time, and life.

wari pal — “water goes”
Poetic meanings: “time flows” / “life moves forward”.

mara can mean soil, earth, land, homeland, or country.

awa a mara — “people and land”
Cultural meaning: “people and homeland” / “the people and the land”.

22. Basic Conversation

A: ti ku-ta pal ya?
Where are you going?

B: ŋa wama-ta pal.
I am going to the sea.

A: ke-ta?
For what purpose?

B: ŋa kana-m man.
I am going to eat fish.

A: kali-sa.
Please come again.

B: sai. ŋa kali.
Okay. I will come.
